Northstar Services
Refund & Exception Policy

Document Owner: Financial Operations & Customer Experience
Applies To: Customer Support, Billing Specialists, Team Leads, Operations Managers
Primary Systems: Northstar Support Desk, PayFlow, Customer Ledger

1. Purpose

The Refund & Exception Policy defines when Northstar Services may return customer payments, issue partial refunds, provide service credits, or make exceptions to normal commercial terms.

Employees should distinguish between a refund, which returns money already charged, and a service credit, which applies value toward future Northstar services.

2. Standard Refund Eligibility

Customers are normally eligible for a full refund when:

- Northstar charged the customer twice for the same service;
- the paid service was never delivered because of a confirmed Northstar failure;
- the customer cancelled within an applicable cooling-off period;
- a billing error caused an amount to be charged incorrectly; or
- the service was cancelled by Northstar before meaningful delivery began.

Refund requests should normally be submitted within 30 calendar days of the original charge.

Requests submitted between 31 and 60 days may still be considered but require Team Lead approval.

Requests more than 60 days after the charge normally require Operations Manager approval.

Requests older than 180 days should generally be denied unless there is evidence of fraud, legal obligation, systemic billing error, or another extraordinary circumstance.

3. Partial Refunds

A partial refund may be appropriate when the customer received part of the purchased service.

Agents should calculate refunds based on undelivered value where reasonably measurable.

For subscriptions, Northstar normally does not provide prorated refunds for partially used billing periods unless:

- a Northstar outage prevented meaningful use for more than 48 consecutive hours;
- the customer was incorrectly billed after confirmed cancellation;
- a contractual agreement requires proration; or
- a Team Lead approves an exception.

A customer who simply stopped using the service is not automatically entitled to a partial refund.

4. Refund Approval Limits

Support Specialists may approve refunds up to $75 per customer case.

Senior Specialists may approve up to $200.

Team Leads may approve up to $750.

Operations Managers may approve refunds up to $5,000.

Amounts above $5,000 require approval from the Director of Operations or Finance.

Approval limits apply to the total financial adjustment associated with the underlying issue, not each individual transaction.

For example, three $70 refunds related to the same billing error equal a $210 adjustment and cannot be individually approved by an employee whose limit is $75.

5. Service Credits

Service credits may be used when a customer experienced inconvenience but a cash refund is not clearly justified.

Standard agents may issue credits up to $50.

Credits should not normally exceed 25% of the affected service value unless approved by a Team Lead.

A service credit should not be offered as a substitute when the customer is legally or contractually entitled to a refund.

Customers may decline a credit and continue requesting a refund.

6. Refund Timing

Approved card refunds are normally submitted to the payment processor within two business days.

The customer may require an additional 3–10 business days to see the refund depending on their financial institution.

Employees must not promise that the refund will appear on a specific date unless the payment processor provides confirmed timing.

Bank transfer refunds may take longer and should generally be completed within 10 business days after approval.

7. Exceptions and Special Circumstances

Northstar may approve refunds outside standard policy when there is a clear service recovery benefit.

Factors that may support an exception include:

- substantial repeat service failures;
- incorrect guidance previously provided by Northstar;
- vulnerability or hardship circumstances;
- a strategic account relationship;
- significant documented operational failure.

Customer dissatisfaction by itself is not sufficient reason to bypass policy.

Team Leads may approve one discretionary exception per customer of up to $250 in a rolling 12-month period.

Additional discretionary exceptions require Operations Manager approval.

8. Suspected Abuse

Employees should not accuse customers of refund abuse.

Cases should be flagged for review when there are:

- three or more refund requests within 90 days;
- inconsistent explanations;
- multiple accounts using the same payment method;
- repeated claims involving services records show were delivered; or
- unusually large refund requests inconsistent with account history.

A fraud flag does not automatically mean a refund must be denied.

Legitimate duplicate charges and confirmed billing errors should still be corrected even when the customer is under review.

9. Ambiguous Scenarios

When both the customer and Northstar contributed to a problem, employees may consider a partial refund or service credit.

Example: A customer missed an appointment because Northstar sent the reminder late, but the customer had separately received the correct appointment time earlier.

There is no mandatory percentage for shared-responsibility cases.

The employee should document reasoning and seek Team Lead approval when the proposed adjustment exceeds their authority.

If a refund policy conflicts with a signed customer contract, the contract normally takes precedence unless the contract provision appears unlawful or the employee has been instructed otherwise by Legal or Finance.

Employees should not interpret uncertain legal obligations themselves. Such cases should be escalated.
