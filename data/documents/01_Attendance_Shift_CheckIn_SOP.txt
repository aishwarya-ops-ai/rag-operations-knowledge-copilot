Northstar Services
Attendance & Shift Check-In SOP

Document Owner: Workforce Operations
Applies To: Front-line associates, reviewers, support specialists, team leads, and contractors working scheduled production shifts
Primary Systems: Northstar Workforce Portal (NWP), TimeTrack, Slack Operations Channels

1. Purpose

The Attendance & Shift Check-In SOP defines how employees are expected to confirm attendance, begin scheduled work, report delays, and handle attendance-related exceptions.

Employees are responsible for reviewing their schedules before each workday. Not receiving a reminder from TimeTrack does not excuse a missed shift.

A scheduled shift is considered successfully started only when the employee completes the required check-in procedure. Logging into Slack, opening company email, or turning on a company-issued computer does not independently count as attendance.

2. Standard Check-In Procedure

Employees may check in through TimeTrack beginning 10 minutes before their scheduled shift start time.

A check-in recorded between 10 minutes before the shift and 5 minutes after the scheduled start time is considered On Time.

Example:

A shift scheduled for 9:00 AM may be checked in between 8:50 AM and 9:05 AM without being classified as late.

Employees checking in 6–15 minutes after the scheduled start time are classified as Late – Level 1.

Employees checking in 16–30 minutes late are classified as Late – Level 2 and must provide a reason in the attendance portal before the end of the shift.

An employee who has not checked in within 30 minutes of the scheduled start is automatically marked Absent – Pending Review, unless the employee or another authorized person has already contacted the Team Lead.

Employees may not check in on behalf of another employee.

3. Technical Check-In Failures

If TimeTrack is unavailable, the employee must post the following in the designated attendance Slack channel:

- employee ID;
- scheduled shift;
- attempted check-in time; and
- screenshot or description of the error.

The Slack message must be submitted within 10 minutes of the scheduled shift start.

The Team Lead may later request Workforce Operations to manually correct the attendance record.

If the employee claims a technical failure but provides no evidence, the Team Lead may still approve a manual correction when system logs independently show an outage.

A personal internet outage is not automatically considered a system outage.

Employees working remotely are expected to maintain a reasonable backup connection where practical. However, outages affecting an entire neighborhood, confirmed ISP disruptions, or electricity failures lasting more than 15 minutes may qualify as an excused technical absence.

4. Late Notification Rules

Employees who know in advance that they will be late should notify their Team Lead as soon as reasonably possible.

When the expected delay exceeds 15 minutes, notification should normally occur before the shift begins.

Notification does not automatically excuse lateness.

A Team Lead may excuse up to two late arrivals of 15 minutes or less within a rolling 30-day period when caused by exceptional circumstances.

A third incident within the same rolling period requires Operations Manager approval to be treated as excused.

Repeated lateness may trigger attendance coaching even when individual incidents were approved.

5. No-Show and Emergency Exceptions

An employee is considered a No-Call No-Show when all of the following apply:

1. no check-in is recorded within 60 minutes of the shift start;
2. the employee has not contacted the Team Lead or Workforce Operations; and
3. no emergency information has been received from another credible source.

One No-Call No-Show requires documented coaching.

Two incidents within 90 days must be escalated to the Operations Manager.

Three incidents within 180 days may be referred to HR for formal disciplinary review.

Emergency circumstances may override this process.

Examples include hospitalization, accidents, immediate family emergencies, or situations where the employee could not reasonably access a phone.

Supporting documentation may be requested but is not mandatory in every emergency.

6. Early Departure

Employees must receive approval before leaving a shift more than 15 minutes early.

Team Leads may approve early departures of up to 60 minutes if operational coverage remains above minimum staffing requirements.

Departures exceeding 60 minutes require Operations Manager approval unless there is an immediate health or safety emergency.

Employees who become sick during a shift should inform their Team Lead before signing out where reasonably possible.

If someone disconnects suddenly and cannot be reached for 30 minutes, the Team Lead should classify the event as Unplanned Disconnect – Review Required, rather than immediately recording an attendance violation.

7. Schedule Disputes and Ambiguous Cases

Employees are expected to follow the schedule displayed in NWP.

However, if a Team Lead communicated a different schedule in writing, such as through email or Slack, the written instruction may override NWP.

Verbal schedule changes are more difficult to verify. In disputes involving verbal instructions, the Operations Manager should review available evidence, including staffing records and statements from both parties.

If NWP displays two overlapping shifts due to a scheduling error, the employee should not be penalized for failing to work both.

Employees working voluntary overtime must still check in through TimeTrack unless the overtime was requested after the shift had already begun.

When the employee is actively working but forgot to check in, Team Leads may approve a retroactive check-in if there is reasonable evidence of work activity. Retroactive corrections submitted more than 72 hours after the shift require Operations Manager approval.

Attendance records are finalized every Monday at 12:00 PM local operations time for the previous week.

Corrections submitted after finalization may still be approved but may not appear until the next payroll cycle.
