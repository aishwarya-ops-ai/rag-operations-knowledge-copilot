Northstar Services
New Employee Training SOP

Document Owner: Learning & Development
Applies To: New Operations Employees, Trainers, Team Leads, Quality Analysts
Primary Systems: Northstar Learning Center (NLC), Training Sandbox, Northstar Quality Hub

1. Purpose

The New Employee Training SOP describes the standard process used to prepare new Northstar Services employees for independent production work.

Training completion does not automatically authorize an employee to perform all production activities. Certain high-risk tasks require separate certification.

2. Standard Training Program

The standard Operations onboarding program lasts 10 business days.

The expected structure is:

Days 1–2: Company orientation, security, systems access, attendance expectations, and basic product knowledge.

Days 3–5: Process instruction, policy walkthroughs, trainer demonstrations, and guided exercises.

Days 6–7: Sandbox case simulations.

Days 8–9: Supervised production or controlled live-case handling.

Day 10: Final assessment and readiness review.

The schedule may be adjusted for holidays, system outages, role complexity, or trainee performance.

A shortened training schedule should not be used solely because production staffing is low.

3. Attendance Requirements

Trainees are expected to attend at least 90% of scheduled training hours.

Missing more than 10% does not automatically mean training failure, but the Trainer and Team Lead must determine whether missed material can reasonably be recovered.

Any employee missing a full day during Days 3–7 should normally complete a makeup session before advancing to supervised production.

Employees missing Day 1 may join the cohort on Day 2 only if required security, HR, and access modules are completed separately.

Employees absent for more than three training days should normally be moved to a later cohort unless an Operations Manager approves continuation.

4. Knowledge Checks

Trainees complete short knowledge checks during the program.

Knowledge checks on Days 3–7 require a recommended score of 80% but may be retaken without formal penalty.

The final assessment requires at least 85%.

Employees scoring 80–84% on the final assessment may receive one remediation session and one reassessment.

Employees scoring below 80% should not proceed directly to production.

A second failed final assessment requires a readiness review involving the Trainer and Team Lead.

A third attempt requires Operations Manager approval.

5. Supervised Production

During supervised production, trainees may handle live cases only under an approved nesting environment.

At least the first five live cases should be reviewed by a Trainer, Team Lead, or certified mentor before final closure where operationally practical.

This requirement may be modified during high-volume periods, but at least five cases must still receive retrospective review within 24 hours.

Trainees must not independently process:

- refunds above $75;
- formal legal complaints;
- Level 3 or Level 4 escalations;
- suspected data-security incidents;
- permanent account closures involving outstanding balances.

These tasks require assistance even if the trainee has technically completed the relevant training module.

6. Nesting Period

After passing formal training, employees enter a five-working-day nesting period.

During nesting, employees normally work at approximately 60–80% of the standard productivity expectation.

Quality expectations are not reduced to the same degree.

Employees are expected to maintain at least an 80% quality score during nesting, increasing to the standard 85% expectation after nesting.

A single failed case does not automatically extend nesting.

Nesting should normally be extended by three to five working days when:

- average quality is below 80%;
- the employee demonstrates repeated policy misunderstanding;
- significant system-navigation problems remain; or
- the employee requires assistance on more than approximately 30% of routine cases by the end of Day 5.

7. Certification and High-Risk Work

Some activities require separate certification.

For example, employees may need individual certification for:

- high-value refunds;
- enterprise accounts;
- financial disputes;
- safety-related escalations;
- quality auditing;
- customer-data export requests.

Completing general onboarding does not grant these permissions.

Managers should not instruct uncertified employees to perform restricted work merely because queues are understaffed.

Emergency temporary authorization may be granted by an Operations Manager for specific low-to-moderate-risk tasks, but the authorization must be documented and should normally last no more than one shift.

8. Trainer and Trainee Disagreement

A Trainer may recommend that an employee is not ready for production even if the employee passes the final assessment.

Examples include serious communication issues, repeated unsafe decision-making, inability to navigate required systems, or consistent dependence on trainer assistance.

However, subjective concerns must be documented with examples.

A trainee should not fail solely because their working style differs from the Trainer's preferences.

When the Trainer and Team Lead disagree on readiness, the Operations Manager makes the final decision.

Quality data, simulation results, attendance, trainer observations, and the complexity of errors should all be considered.

9. Ambiguous and Exceptional Cases

A trainee who makes one Critical Error during training is not automatically disqualified.

The Trainer should evaluate whether the error resulted from misunderstanding, unclear training material, system design, or reckless disregard of policy.

Repeated Critical Errors after coaching require escalation to the Operations Manager.

Strong trainees may complete some modules ahead of schedule but should not normally graduate before Day 8 unless they have substantial prior Northstar experience and receive Operations Manager approval.

Former Northstar employees rehired within six months may receive abbreviated training if they return to substantially the same role. Rehires returning after more than six months normally complete at least a skills assessment and policy refresher.

Policies may change between training and production. Employees are responsible for following the current approved policy, even when older training material says something different.

When a trainee identifies a genuine conflict between training material and a live policy document, the Trainer should pause scoring on the disputed issue until the correct guidance is confirmed.
