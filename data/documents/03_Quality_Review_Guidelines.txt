Northstar Services
Quality Review Guidelines

Document Owner: Quality Operations
Applies To: Quality Analysts, Reviewers, Team Leads, Operations Managers
Primary Systems: Northstar Quality Hub (NQH)

1. Purpose

The Quality Review Guidelines establish how completed customer interactions and operational cases are evaluated for accuracy, compliance, process adherence, communication quality, and customer impact.

Quality scores are intended to identify performance patterns. They should not be used as the sole basis for disciplinary decisions without contextual review.

2. Standard Review Volume

Each active front-line employee should normally receive five quality reviews per calendar month.

At least:

- two should be randomly selected;
- one should involve a complex or higher-risk case where available; and
- one should come from the employee's most recent 10 working days.

Employees who handled fewer than 20 eligible cases during the month may receive fewer than five reviews.

Employees in formal quality coaching may receive up to 10 reviews per month.

Quality Analysts should not intentionally select only poor-performing cases unless conducting a targeted investigation.

3. Scoring Framework

Cases are scored across five categories:

- Process Accuracy – 30%
- Resolution Accuracy – 30%
- Communication – 15%
- Documentation – 15%
- Efficiency – 10%

The standard passing score is 85%.

Scores of 80–84.99% are classified as Needs Improvement.

Scores below 80% are classified as Fail.

However, certain errors are defined as Critical Errors and cause the entire review to fail regardless of the numerical score.

Critical Errors include:

- unauthorized disclosure of protected customer information;
- processing a financial adjustment above the employee's authority;
- deliberately falsifying case documentation;
- closing a case without required action where the omission creates material customer or compliance risk; or
- failing to escalate a credible immediate safety threat.

4. Major and Minor Errors

Not every incorrect action is critical.

A Major Error materially affects resolution but does not independently create severe compliance or safety exposure.

Examples include giving a customer the wrong eligibility decision, failing to complete a required refund, or providing incorrect instructions that require another contact.

A Minor Error has limited impact.

Examples include small formatting mistakes, non-material documentation omissions, or failing to use the preferred greeting when the interaction remains otherwise professional.

Three repeated minor errors of the same type within one reviewed case may be treated as one major error if together they materially reduce the quality of the interaction.

5. Reviewer Disagreement

When an employee disputes a review, they may submit a quality appeal within five calendar days of receiving the result.

The original reviewer should first confirm that no obvious scoring error occurred.

If disagreement remains, the case should be assigned to a second Quality Analyst who did not participate in the original review.

If the second reviewer changes the score by less than five percentage points, the second score becomes final unless a critical-error classification is disputed.

If scores differ by five or more percentage points, the case must be sent to a Team Lead or Senior Quality Analyst for adjudication.

All disputed Critical Errors require adjudication regardless of score difference.

6. Gold-Standard and Calibration Reviews

Northstar periodically uses pre-scored gold-standard cases to test reviewer accuracy.

Quality Analysts are expected to maintain at least 90% agreement with approved gold labels across a rolling sample of 20 calibration decisions.

Analysts falling between 80% and 89.99% should receive calibration coaching.

Agreement below 80% should result in temporary removal from independent production reviews until recalibration is completed.

A disagreement with a gold label is not automatically considered reviewer failure. If multiple experienced reviewers independently challenge the gold label, the Quality Manager may reopen the gold-standard decision.

7. Handling Ambiguous Cases

Reviewers must score based on the information reasonably available to the employee at the time the work was completed.

Information added to an account afterward should not normally be used to penalize the employee.

For example, if a customer later provides documentation showing that a refund was invalid, an employee who approved the refund based on valid information available earlier should not automatically fail the review.

Policy language that allows judgment should not be scored as though only one acceptable decision existed.

When two actions are both reasonably defensible under policy, reviewers should generally avoid assigning a major error solely because they personally prefer one approach.

8. Quality Performance Escalation

An employee with an average monthly score below 85% should receive documented coaching.

Two consecutive months below 85% require a formal improvement plan unless insufficient review volume makes the score unreliable.

Any employee receiving two Critical Errors within 30 days requires Operations Manager review.

Three Critical Errors within a rolling 90-day period must be escalated to Senior Operations and HR, although escalation does not automatically mean termination.

Quality metrics should be considered together with case complexity, workload, tenure, and reviewer consistency.
