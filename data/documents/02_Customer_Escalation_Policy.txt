Northstar Services
Customer Escalation Policy

Document Owner: Customer Experience Operations
Applies To: Customer Support Specialists, Escalation Specialists, Team Leads, Operations Managers
Primary Systems: Northstar Support Desk (NSD), CRM, CallHub

1. Purpose

The Customer Escalation Policy defines when a customer issue should remain with the assigned support agent and when it must be transferred, escalated, or reported to higher levels of the organization.

An escalation is based primarily on risk and authority required, not simply customer dissatisfaction.

A customer asking for a supervisor does not automatically mean the issue qualifies as a Level 3 escalation.

2. Escalation Levels

Northstar uses four operational escalation levels.

Level 1 – Standard Resolution

Routine questions, minor complaints, basic account issues, and requests that fall within the agent's approved authority.

Agents should attempt reasonable resolution before escalating.

Level 2 – Team Lead Review

Level 2 applies when:

- the customer requests an exception outside standard policy;
- an agent requires approval above their financial authority;
- the same customer has contacted Northstar three or more times regarding the same unresolved issue within seven days;
- the customer explicitly asks for a supervisor after the agent has attempted resolution; or
- the potential customer impact is estimated between $250 and $1,000.

Level 2 escalations should normally be reviewed within 60 minutes during staffed operating hours.

Level 3 – Operations Manager Escalation

Level 3 applies when:

- potential financial exposure exceeds $1,000;
- more than 10 customers appear to be affected by the same operational failure;
- a corporate client threatens contract cancellation;
- an issue has remained unresolved for more than 48 hours despite Level 2 involvement;
- a customer alleges discriminatory treatment by a Northstar employee; or
- a serious service failure has generated public social-media attention.

Level 3 cases should be acknowledged within 30 minutes when identified during operating hours.

Level 4 – Critical Incident

Level 4 cases include credible threats involving:

- immediate physical safety;
- substantial data exposure;
- law-enforcement involvement;
- major media inquiries;
- regulatory agencies;
- widespread system failure affecting more than approximately 100 active customers.

Employees should immediately notify the Operations Manager and the designated Incident Response contact.

Agents should not investigate Level 4 incidents independently beyond collecting necessary initial information.

3. Customer Threats and Aggressive Language

A customer saying, "I'm going to sue you," does not automatically constitute Level 4.

General legal threats should normally be treated as Level 2 unless the customer identifies an active lawsuit, attorney communication, regulatory filing, or formal legal notice.

Similarly, statements such as "I'll post this everywhere" normally remain Level 2.

However, if a post has already received significant public attention, media engagement, or identifiable reputational impact, the matter may qualify for Level 3.

Threats of violence must be treated differently.

A vague statement made in frustration should be documented and reviewed by a Team Lead.

A specific threat identifying a person, location, method, or timeframe should be escalated immediately as Level 4.

4. Escalation Ownership

The employee who creates an escalation remains responsible for documenting the issue until ownership has been formally accepted by the next escalation level.

Sending a Slack message does not automatically transfer ownership.

The receiving Lead or Manager must acknowledge the case in NSD or explicitly confirm ownership.

If no acknowledgment is received within the required response time, the employee should follow up once.

For Level 3 or Level 4 issues, failure to receive a response within 15 minutes should trigger escalation to the next available manager.

5. Financial Authority

Standard agents may issue service credits up to $50 when supported by policy.

Senior agents may approve up to $150.

Team Leads may approve up to $500.

Amounts between $500 and $2,000 require Operations Manager approval.

Amounts above $2,000 generally require Director approval unless an emergency incident procedure grants temporary authority.

Splitting a refund or credit into multiple transactions to avoid an approval threshold is prohibited.

6. Difficult or Ambiguous Cases

Customer emotion alone does not determine escalation level.

A customer may be extremely angry about a $10 issue that remains Level 1, while a calm customer reporting possible data exposure may require Level 4 escalation.

VIP customers do not automatically receive Level 3 classification. However, enterprise accounts identified as Strategic Accounts should have their Account Manager notified of Level 2 or higher issues.

If multiple escalation rules apply, employees should use the highest applicable level.

If the employee is genuinely uncertain whether a matter qualifies as Level 3 or Level 4, the employee should escalate to Level 3 and notify the Operations Manager immediately rather than delaying the case while attempting to classify it perfectly.

All Level 3 and Level 4 escalations require a written incident summary before case closure.
